Built out of frustration.

I started Brink while studying cyber security, because I wanted a proper browser I could carry in place of my laptop.

Hi, I'm Josh.

I needed a browser that worked with me, not against me.

I'm an independent developer and cybersecurity student based in the UK. When I started studying, I got tired of taking my laptop everywhere. An iPad was easy to carry, but I still needed a browser I could do real research in.

I wanted one place to save things, build citations and keep everything organised, without a notes app, a PDF app and a reference tool all running alongside it.

So I built it. Everything in Brink is there because I wanted it while studying, and it's the browser I use every day.

I study while working full time, so I don't have spare hours to spend managing tools. Brink is meant to take that part off your plate.

Why a browser?

A browser is where research starts, so that's where I put the tools.

When you study, you read an article, save it, make a note, pull out a quote and write the reference. In most setups that means four different apps. In Brink it all happens in one place.

Each assignment or module can have its own project, with its own folders and saved material. Anything that doesn't belong to a project goes in General.

Brink only runs on iPad. I built it around the way I use mine, and I wanted it designed for that screen, not stretched up from a phone.

“I built the browser I wish I'd had when I started studying. Every feature exists because I needed it myself.”
Josh Bradley · Developer of Brink Browser

What I want to keep as Brink grows

Every new feature should earn its place. I would rather Brink stay useful than try to do everything.

Your research stays on your device

Your browsing history, searches, projects, saved pages, notes and lecture recordings aren't collected by Brink. Limited analytics and diagnostics help me fix crashes and improve the app. What you make stays on your device.

Built for research

Projects, folders, citations, Reader Mode, PDFs, notes and recording are there to help with research. If a feature doesn't do that, it doesn't go in.

Room to focus

Focus sessions, Study Mode and website blocking are there for when you need to get something finished.

I read the feedback

Brink is independently developed. The feedback I get decides what I improve, simplify or build next.

One developer. One paid app.

Brink has no investors and no ads. It's paid for by the people who buy it, which is what keeps it independent.

Your £4.99 helps fund development, maintenance and improvements.
